Who Is Tina Peters?

She was a county clerk in a quiet Colorado town. Then she became the first election official in American history convicted of crimes tied to overturning a presidential election. This is the full story.

Updated May 2026 ~25 min read 9 sections
i. A Quiet County

YOUR OPENING PARAGRAPH GOES HERE. Grand Junction sits at the confluence of the Colorado and Gunnison rivers, three hours from Denver and a world away from it. Introduce Mesa County — the landscape, the politics, the conservatism. Make the reader feel the place before they meet the person.

CONTINUE THE SCENE HERE. Introduce Tina Peters as she was in 2018: 63 years old, political newcomer, former flight attendant, ran a construction business with her husband. Campaigned on reopening DMV satellite offices. Nothing about her suggested what was coming.

She won the 2018 Republican primary for Mesa County Clerk and Recorder with 53% of the vote. Her opponent had worked in the clerk's office for ten years. Peters had never worked there a day in her life.

ii. The Job She Wasn't Ready For

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. Cover her troubled early tenure. The 574 uncounted ballots found in a parking lot drop box in February 2020. The recall attempt that summer — which failed by only 1,200 signatures. Former employees filing complaints. The fact that she never completed the certification required of a county clerk.

She was already embattled, already under scrutiny, before the 2020 election arrived. That context matters.

iii. April 23, 2021

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. This is the pivot section — the most important date in the story. Prosecutors called this meeting "the beginning of the conspiracy." Douglas Frank arrives in Grand Junction, paid by Mike Lindell. Sherronna Bishop brokers the meeting. Walk the reader through what happened.

"I would be glad to help you find phantom ballots. I've been going around the country helping lots of people, and Mike Lindell pays me to do that."

Douglas Frank  ·  Recorded April 23, 2021  ·  Mesa County

Peters had signed documents certifying no discrepancies in Mesa County's 2020 election results. After April 23, she would never publicly say so again.

iv. The Trusted Build

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. The mechanics of the crime, told narratively. How she created fake credentials for Gerald Wood. How Conan Hayes — a retired surfer with ties to Lindell — came to be standing in a secure government facility copying hard drives. How the data leaked online. How Secretary of State Griswold discovered it.

v. The Circus

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. Peters flies to Lindell's cyber symposium while her office is being raided. She drops her reelection bid and runs for Secretary of State instead — on an election-fraud platform. She loses. She demands a recount. She loses the recount. The Selection Code documentary casts her as a hero. She becomes a fixture on right-wing media.

This section has a slightly faster pace — the reader should feel the escalation, the absurdity of each new move.

vi. The Trial

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. The trial itself, chronologically. Opening statements. The prosecution's case. The testimony of Belinda Knisley and Sandra Brown — the deputies who flipped. The evidence the jury was allowed to see, and what was kept out. Peters never testified. The verdict: guilty on 7 of 10 charges. She went on Steve Bannon's War Room podcast the next day.

vii. You Are No Hero

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. The sentencing hearing in full. Her 40-minute statement. The photos of her son and her ex-husband. The accusations against DA Rubinstein. The judge's response. The handcuffs. The $1.4 million cost to Mesa County. The election workers who received death threats. The community that called itself a laughingstock.

Mesa County District Court  ·  October 3, 2024  ·  Sentencing Hearing
Judge "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."
Peters "I've never done anything with malice to break the law. I've only wanted to serve the people of Mesa County."
Judge "I am convinced you would do it all over again if you could. You're as defiant as any defendant this court has ever seen."
Peters "If you tell me not to talk about the election, I won't talk about the election."

She was handcuffed and taken to the Mesa County Detention Center immediately after sentencing. Nine years. Her attorneys said they would appeal.

viii. The Machine Keeps Running

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. Life after sentencing. Trump's symbolic pardon that meant nothing legally. Colorado's resistance. Federal funding threats. The appeals court upholding her conviction in April 2026 but ordering resentencing — finding that the judge had violated her free speech rights by calling her a charlatan. The legal paradox at the heart of her case.

ix. "Free Tina"

YOUR CLIMAX GOES HERE. May 15, 2026. Polis commutes her sentence. She reportedly told him she "made a mistake" — a stark reversal from her defiant courtroom performance. Trump posts "FREE TINA." The DA who prosecuted her, a Republican, condemns the commutation as special treatment for the powerful. The Secretary of State says it will embolden election deniers for years.

Bobbie Gross, who now holds Peters' old job, is still rebuilding trust with 120,000 voters one conversation at a time.

End in present tense: As of this writing, Tina Peters is eligible for parole on June 1, 2026. She may already be free.
