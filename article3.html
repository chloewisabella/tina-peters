Who Is Tina Peters to Those Who Know Her?

The allies, the patrons, the believers — and the conspiracy they built around her. Operation Sacrificial Lamb, Mike Lindell, and the machinery of election denial.

~25 min read 6 sections Includes composite interviews
i. The Patron

In the months after the Mesa County election security breach became public, Tina Peters did not lack for resources. Her legal defense was funded. Her story was filmed. Her travel was covered. Behind most of it was one person: MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell.

Campaign finance complaints filed against Peters and later substantiated by the Colorado Secretary of State's office showed she had received significant financial support from Lindell and affiliated organizations. As an elected official, Peters was prohibited under Colorado law from receiving gifts. The violations contributed to a $15,400 fine levied against her in October 2023.

Lindell also paid for Douglas Frank's travel to Grand Junction for the April 23, 2021 meeting that prosecutors called the beginning of the conspiracy. A secret recording of that meeting, played at Peters' trial, captured Frank telling those present that Lindell funded his nationwide tour of county election offices.

In 2022, Lindell produced a documentary about Peters called Selection Code. The film cast Peters as a whistleblower who had accidentally discovered evidence of a global election manipulation scheme. It did not mention Conan Hayes, the unauthorized person Peters had smuggled into the trusted build. It did not mention that the copied data had been leaked online. Reviewers noted the film's claims had not held up against subsequent reporting and legal proceedings.

Lindell also flew Peters to his "cyber symposium" in Sioux Falls, South Dakota in August 2021 — the event she attended while her office was being raided by federal investigators.

Daniel Mercer, a campaign finance researcher who has tracked election-denial funding networks, said Lindell's investment in Peters followed a pattern seen across multiple states.

"Lindell identified local officials who were sympathetic to his claims and provided them with resources — legal support, media platforms, travel," Mercer said. "Peters was one of the most prominent examples, but she wasn't the only one. The model was to find someone with institutional access and amplify them."

What Lindell received in return was content. Peters' story — a local clerk stumbling onto evidence of a global conspiracy — was exactly the kind of narrative his movement needed. Her institutional access, her willingness to go public and her subsequent prosecution gave the election-denial movement a martyr with a paper trail.

Sheila Rae, the former Mesa County Clerk who succeeded Peters temporarily after her removal, said the financial relationship was an open secret in Grand Junction.

"She gets paid for those podcasts," Rae said. "It's on the books that she received money from Lindell and that entire group. She also accepted private jet rides from him. Who knows how much she actually received from donations — she's constantly raising money."

ii. The Brokers

Tina Peters did not act alone. The network surrounding her included a former congressional campaign manager, a traveling mathematician, a retired surfer with connections to a separate election breach in Georgia and a Florida attorney who drafted a model executive order for the Trump White House.

Sherronna Bishop, who brokered the April 23, 2021 meeting between Peters and Douglas Frank, had managed Lauren Boebert's successful 2020 congressional campaign in Colorado's 3rd District. She ran a Facebook livestream called "The Patriot Call" and had established relationships with national election-denial figures before approaching Peters.

Douglas Frank, the Ohio mathematician Lindell paid to travel the country, provided Peters with what supporters described as mathematical proof of election fraud. His methodology was rejected by professional statisticians and election officials who reviewed his work. He continued presenting it regardless and appeared at Peters' sentencing in October 2024 to speak on her behalf.

"I am uniquely qualified to judge Tina Peters' state of mind," Frank told the court. "It boggles my mind that a Gold Star mother is subjected to this tyranny."

Conan James Hayes, the retired California surfer Peters smuggled into the trusted build using Gerald Wood's credentials, was later linked by investigators to a similar breach of election equipment in Coffee County, Georgia. The parallel operation used the same general approach — unauthorized access to Dominion voting machines during a maintenance procedure — and involved some of the same people. The connection suggested the Mesa County breach was not improvised but part of a coordinated national effort.

Peter Ticktin, Peters' defense attorney, drafted a model executive order for President Trump in coordination with Michael Flynn and Patrick Byrne that would have directed federal resources toward investigating election fraud claims. The order was never signed. The drafting placed Peters' legal team at the highest levels of the post-2020 election-denial movement.

Sarah Voss, a former Mesa County Republican campaign worker, said the network around Peters was more organized than it appeared from the outside.

"These weren't random people who found each other," Voss said. "Bishop knew Frank. Frank knew Lindell. Lindell knew Ticktin. Peters was the local access point they needed. She may have believed she was running her own operation, but she was also being run."

Voss said the structure mirrored other far-right organizing efforts from the same period.

"You had a national funding source, a traveling field operation and local officials who provided institutional legitimacy," Voss said. "It's a model. Mesa County was one node in a much larger network."

iii. Operation Sacrificial Lamb

Among Peters' supporters, her prosecution has a name: Operation Sacrificial Lamb.

The term does not appear in any official court document or law enforcement record. It originated in the right-wing media ecosystem that grew up around Peters' case and was adopted by her supporters as a shorthand for a specific theory: that Peters was deliberately targeted by state and federal officials to make an example of her and discourage other election officials from questioning voting systems.

Dee Valdez Pepper, a Republican activist from Elbert County who became one of Peters' closest supporters, laid out the theory in an interview.

"This was an operation that the county commissioners put in place in Mesa County to frame Tina Peters," Valdez Pepper said. "So that they could put the fear of God in all the clerks around the country and prevent anyone from asking any questions."

Valdez Pepper said evidence for the operation came partly from a Mesa County commissioner meeting she claimed was secretly recorded. She said her husband used audio software to isolate a whispered exchange between commissioners that she believes contained the phrase "sacrificial lamb" — used as a trigger word to initiate a search warrant against Peters.

Mesa County Commissioner Cody Davis, who spoke at Peters' sentencing and called her actions an embarrassment to the community, has not publicly responded to the sacrificial lamb allegation. The Grand Junction Daily Sentinel found no evidence supporting the account.

Valdez Pepper said she believes the prosecution extended to the highest levels of state government. She named Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold, Attorney General Phil Weiser and Judge Matthew Barrett as participants in the operation.

"They chose Tina to be the sacrificial lamb," Valdez Pepper said. "They needed somebody to be the example."

The theory has circulated widely in right-wing media. Joe Hoft's Gateway Pundit published multiple pieces framing Peters' incarceration as political persecution. A video titled "Sacrificial Lamb: The Tina Peters Story" was posted to Truth Social in January 2026. Trump's "FREE TINA" posts fit neatly into the same narrative.

Griswold, who launched the investigation into the breach after the copied data appeared online, rejected the characterization.

"The only person who created this situation is Tina Peters," Griswold said following Peters' conviction. "She made choices that compromised the security of Mesa County's election system and harmed voters and election workers across the state."

Rae, who helped run the office after Peters was removed, said the theory inverts the actual sequence of events.

"The Mesa County elections were tabulated correctly and had been audited in a number of ways," Rae said. "The State of Colorado has some of the most stringent auditing requirements in the nation, and the 2020 elections passed every single audit and more. The big lie is to say that any of our elections were fraudulent or manipulated in any way."

"After April of 2021 when Tina accidentally discovered the 'smoking gun' of how the machines orchestrate the integral part in the larger global network designed to manipulate and steal elections, there was no turning back — not for Tina, nor for the Deep State."
