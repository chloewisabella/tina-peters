The allies, the patrons, the believers — and the conspiracy they built around her. Operation Sacrificial Lamb, Mike Lindell, and the machinery of election denial.

i. The Patron

YOUR OPENING GOES HERE. Mike Lindell — MyPillow CEO, the most prominent funder and amplifier of election denial in America — is the central figure in Peters' outer world. He didn't just pay her legal bills. He paid Douglas Frank to travel the country and find people like her. He produced the documentary that made her famous. He flew her to his symposium. He gave her a narrative, a platform, and an audience. Who used whom?

Explore the patron-client relationship. Peters became Lindell's content. Her story — local clerk discovers global conspiracy — was exactly the kind of hero narrative his movement needed. She gave him credibility. He gave her purpose. It's possible both things are true simultaneously.

ii. The Brokers

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. Sherronna Bishop brought Douglas Frank to Peters. Douglas Frank brought the mathematics. Conan Hayes — a retired surfer — brought the technical execution. Peter Ticktin brought the legal strategy, and then went further: drafting a model executive order for Trump coordinating with Michael Flynn and Patrick Byrne. Each person in Peters' circle was connected to a broader national network. She was not a lone actor. She was a node.

iii. Operation Sacrificial Lamb

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. "Operation Sacrificial Lamb" is not an official operation name. It is the retroactive mythology her supporters built to explain her prosecution. In this telling: Peters is the lone honest clerk who stumbled across evidence of a global election manipulation scheme. Her prosecution is a Deep State operation to silence a whistleblower and make an example of her — to frighten other potential truth-tellers. Her suffering is deliberate persecution.

"After April of 2021 when Tina accidentally discovered the 'smoking gun' of how the machines orchestrate the integral part in the larger global network designed to manipulate and steal elections, there was no turning back — not for Tina, nor for the Deep State."

Right-wing blog post, 2026  ·  Representative of the "Sacrificial Lamb" narrative

Explore the closed epistemic loop of conspiratorial thinking: every piece of counter-evidence becomes further proof of the conspiracy's reach. Her conviction proved they were out to get her. The judge's harsh words proved political persecution. The appeals court's partial vindication proved she was right all along.

YOUR TEXT GOES HERE. Draw on your interview with Bobbie Gross — the woman who now holds Peters' old job. Gross is a Republican. She worked in the Mesa County clerk's office since 2007. She was Peters' opponent in the 2018 primary — the race Peters won. She was elected clerk in 2023, after Peters was removed, and has spent her first year rebuilding trust with 120,000 voters one conversation at a time.

What does Gross make of Peters? What was the office like when she took it over? What does the damage look like from the inside? This interview is the ground-level view of what election denial actually costs — not in the abstract, but in a specific community, measured in specific broken relationships.

vi. The Family Dissenters

YOUR CLOSING GOES HERE. Jack Peters — Tom's brother, Tina's former brother-in-law — is a self-described "extremely conservative" Republican who nevertheless called her "vile" in print. He said she was abrasive. He said she exploited Remington's death for donations. He said her claimed role running Tom's construction company amounted to "part-time bookkeeping." He is not a liberal critic. He is a conservative family member who watched her up close.

Close with what the sum of all these perspectives adds up to. Not a verdict — a portrait. The allies who elevated her, the community she damaged, the family member who saw through her, the successor cleaning up her mess, the friend who still believes in her.
